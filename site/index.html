The problem

Your address book stopped telling the truth years ago.

The people who matter are buried.

Five thousand names, and the ones you'd most like to hear from are the ones you haven't written to in years. Scrolling won't find them.

It lives in accounts you're losing.

The company you left, the one you sold, the one shutting down in March. Each holds a slice of who you know, and the slice goes when the account does.

A lot of it is dead.

Old work addresses, acquired companies, people who moved on. Nothing tells you which until a note bounces.

How it works

Your AI does the sorting. You do the deciding.

Act one · minutes

Reconnect

  • Reads email headers only: who, when, how often. Not what you wrote.
  • Measures relationships by back-and-forth over time, not volume. A friend you wrote 40 times outranks a colleague's 4,000 work emails.
  • Leaves out the transactions (lawyers, vendors, recruiters) and gives you 10 to 15 people worth writing to.
Act two · optional

Clean up

  • Pulls every account's contacts into one list, in Google's own import format.
  • Removes dead addresses from bounces and "no longer with the company" replies, and keeps the new address when one is given.
  • Folds duplicates only when the names agree too, so an office switchboard never turns ten colleagues into one card.

Anything about a person comes to you, once, in one batch.

Same person or two? Keep or let go? Family and anyone sensitive stay exactly as you say. Every answer, and every automatic change, goes into a change log with its reason. Try it.

No server of ours

It runs inside your own assistant, on files and accounts you connect. We never see your contacts. Your AI provider does, so use one you trust with your email.

Everything on the record

Every removal, merge and label is logged with its reason. Your original exports are copied first and never touched.

Nothing you can't undo

The import is tested on five contacts first, and Google's Undo changes puts everything back. Nobody gets cut without your say.

Why reconnect

The people you've lost touch with are often the most useful to find again.

In studies of executives who got back in touch with people they hadn't spoken to in years, those conversations took less time than talks with current contacts and were rated as useful or more so. Old ties bring fresh information and still carry trust.

Levin, Walter & Murnighan, "Dormant Ties: The Value of Reconnecting," Organization Science, 2011.

Questions

Do you see my contacts or email?

No. There's no Quaiint server. Everything goes between you, your assistant and your accounts. Your AI provider sees what you give your assistant.

Does it read my emails?

Only the headers: who, to whom, when. It asks before using subject lines, and never reads message bodies unless you ask.

Can it delete people?

No. It removes dead addresses and logs each one. Cutting a person is always your call, and the import is yours to click.

What does it cost?

The skill is free and open source. Your assistant's normal usage applies.

How often should I run it?

Once a quarter: a fresh reconnect list, and a list that stays current.
